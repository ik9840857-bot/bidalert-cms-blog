Procurement in India: Navigating the Complexities of GST and Regulatory Compliance

Published: 2026-02-05

Introduction to Procurement in India

Procurement in India has become an increasingly complex process, with the implementation of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) and various regulatory compliance requirements. The Indian government has introduced several initiatives to simplify and streamline the procurement process, but businesses still face numerous challenges. In this article, we will explore the complexities of procurement in India, with a focus on GST and regulatory compliance.

Understanding GST and its Impact on Procurement

The introduction of GST in 2017 was a significant milestone in India's taxation history. GST is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax that has replaced multiple indirect taxes, including excise duty, service tax, and value-added tax. While GST has simplified the taxation process to some extent, it has also introduced new complexities in procurement. Businesses must now navigate the nuances of GST, including input tax credits, reverse charge mechanisms, and GST rates for various goods and services.

Regulatory Compliance in Procurement

In addition to GST, businesses in India must comply with various regulatory requirements, including the Public Procurement Order, 2017, and the General Financial Rules, 2017. These regulations aim to promote transparency, accountability, and fairness in public procurement. However, they also add to the complexity of the procurement process, with requirements such as:

  • Registration with the Central Public Procurement Portal
  • Obtaining a Unique Identification Number (UIN)
  • Compliance with the Make in India initiative
  • Adherence to the General Financial Rules, 2017

Challenges in Procurement in India

Despite the efforts to simplify and streamline the procurement process, businesses in India still face numerous challenges. Some of the key challenges include:

  • Lack of standardization in procurement processes
  • Difficulty in navigating the complexities of GST and regulatory compliance
  • Delays in payment and settlement of invoices
  • Corruption and lack of transparency in public procurement

Best Practices for Procurement in India

To navigate the complexities of procurement in India, businesses must adopt best practices, including:

  • Implementing a robust procurement strategy that takes into account GST and regulatory compliance requirements
  • Developing a comprehensive understanding of the procurement process and the various regulations and laws that govern it
  • Utilizing technology and digital platforms to streamline the procurement process and improve transparency and efficiency
  • Engaging with experts and consultants who have experience in procurement in India

Conclusion

In conclusion, procurement in India is a complex process that requires a deep understanding of GST and regulatory compliance requirements. Businesses must navigate the nuances of GST, comply with various regulatory requirements, and adopt best practices to ensure a smooth and efficient procurement process.
